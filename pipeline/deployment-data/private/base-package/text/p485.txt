Water vapour diffusion
The air in a room, and also the air in general, contain water in the
form of water vapour. The quantity of water in the air, depending on
temperature, is called the relative humidity. It is important to note that
warm air can absorb much more water vapour than cold air. The relative
humidity can therefore fluctuate greatly according to the temperature
conditions, although the absolute quantity of water remains constant.
For the formation of condensation, an important factor is that the
relative air humidity increases with falling temperatures. In
extreme cases, this can be so pronounced that the air is no longer
capable of holding the water in the form of vapour and it is then
deposited as condensation.
